# Q-Neuro Model Card

## Model details

Q-Neuro is an experimental family of small sequence models that evolves real, paired-real,
complex, energy, Hamiltonian-inspired, or density-like hypothesis states as evidence arrives. The
current reference checkpoint is the approximately 20k-real-scalar complex operator trained in
QN-000014. There is no production endpoint.

## Intended use

- Research on inductive biases for ordered evidence, uncertainty, causal synthetic shifts,
  active feature acquisition, and alternative learning laws.
- Reproduction and critique of the included NeuroWorld studies.
- Teaching examples for ablation design, negative-result reporting, and experiment provenance.

## Out-of-scope use

- Clinical diagnosis, triage, treatment, prognosis, patient communication, or medical advice.
- Evaluation of a real person, medical record, scan, EEG, laboratory result, or symptom list.
- Claims about quantum mechanics in brains or medicine.
- Claims of external validity, demographic fairness, safety, or regulatory fitness.

## Data

All training and evaluation cases are programmatically generated by NeuroWorld. The simulator uses
20 abstract diagnosis archetypes and 40 binary findings with explicit missingness, causal factors,
temporal stages, and controlled world shifts. No private, patient-derived, or clinical record data
are included.

## Evaluated behavior

- The complex operator shows a repeatable 0.054–0.063 top-1 advantage over the tested
  two-channel real control across four shift severities and five unseen NeuroWorld seeds.
- The same model is poorly calibrated on constructed irreducible ambiguity (pair NLL 2.581 versus
  1.148 for a real operator).
- Conventional controls establish important boundaries: a tuned GRU is strongest in the lowest-
  data source regime and generally yields more linearly readable simulator factors.
- Local, phase-gradient, density-rank, dissipative, and adaptive-depth proposals do not establish a
  broad improvement under the tested conditions.

## Limitations and risks

The simulator encodes the researchers’ assumptions, disease names are abstract, sample sizes at
the world level are small, and no external dataset is evaluated. Excellent synthetic OOD AUROC can
reflect easy separation rather than useful novelty detection. High confidence may persist under
shift, and source-fitted temperature scaling can worsen shifted calibration. Apparent explanations
are computation traces, not validated clinical reasoning.

## Safe handling

Treat every prediction as a synthetic benchmark measurement. Do not enter personal medical data.
Do not expose a checkpoint behind a medical-facing interface. Report all results with the safety
banner: **RESEARCH PROTOTYPE — NOT FOR CLINICAL USE.**
