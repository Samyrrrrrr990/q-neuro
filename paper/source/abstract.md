# Abstract

Structured complex-valued networks are often evaluated against real-valued controls that share an output format but not the candidate model's exact computation. We show how this comparator choice can reverse a scientific conclusion. In an initial synthetic chronology benchmark, a complex recurrent operator exceeded a two-channel real control by 0.0602 top-1 accuracy under moderate shift (five worlds; interval 0.0529 to 0.0674). We then introduced the structured real block that exactly implements complex multiplication, expanded the comparison to a cellwise best-real envelope, and prospectively separated discovery from untouched task families. The exact real block reproduced the complex model's predictions in all 1,920 held-out family-world-seed-severity cells, with maximum NLL difference 3.58 x 10^-7. In reduced independent-task discovery, none of 2,880 complex-minus-best-real effects was positive (mean -0.0370). In four untouched generator families, none of 1,920 effects was positive; the family/world/seed hierarchical mean was -0.00916 (95% bootstrap interval -0.01325 to -0.00457). A quadratic relationship selected before outcome access fit 12 discovery aggregates (R2 0.949; MAE 0.00260) but failed unchanged on 12 held-out aggregates (R2 -30.94; MAE 0.0313). The single-use grand experiment was not executed because six mandatory readiness gates failed, and its sealed benchmark remains unopened. These studies do not show that complex parameterizations are universally ineffective. They falsify the narrower claim that the implemented complex arithmetic has an intrinsic robustness advantage over its exact and strongest tested real controls. The result is synthetic, compute-reduced, nonclinical, and not evidence of medical utility or quantum cognition. The primary contribution is a preregistered, artifact-backed workflow for converting an apparent architectural advantage into a falsifiable comparator claim.
