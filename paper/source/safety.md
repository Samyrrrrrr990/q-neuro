# Safety and responsible use

## Nonclinical boundary

Q-Neuro is a computational research repository. It has not been trained or evaluated on patient records, has no diagnostic indication, and provides no estimate of sensitivity, specificity, patient benefit, fairness, robustness in care settings, or clinical safety. Disease-like names in NeuroWorld are fictional task labels. The independent families are likewise synthetic, including the machine-fault and network-intrusion metaphors.

The repository and manuscript must not be presented as a medical device, clinical decision support system, or validated diagnostic model. Any future patient-data work would require separate governance, ethics review, privacy protections, clinical experts, external validation, prospective evaluation, and regulatory analysis. It could not retroactively validate the present synthetic claims.

## Quantum-language boundary

Complex amplitudes, phase, interference-like readout, density matrices, and noncommuting updates are mathematical tools. Their use does not demonstrate quantum hardware, quantum speedup, quantum biology, or a quantum mechanism of human judgment. The code runs on ordinary classical hardware. Claims are worded in terms of implemented computations and observed synthetic metrics.

## Misuse-resistant release

The release places limitations and failed claims in machine-readable ledgers, makes the negative result the first dashboard finding, and reports the unopened grand benchmark prominently. The paper retains the historical positive estimate only with its comparator identity. These measures reduce the risk that a single favorable number is detached from the control audit.

## Required future validation

Before any operational domain is considered, a future protocol must freeze a real dataset version and license, environment split, target, exclusions, leakage tests, comparator envelope, compute budget, primary metric, uncertainty method, and stop rule before final labels are accessed. Clinical domains additionally require domain-specific safety analysis and cannot be treated as ordinary benchmark extensions.
