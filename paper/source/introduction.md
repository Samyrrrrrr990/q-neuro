# Introduction

Many machine-learning systems for diagnosis are trained as static mappings from a completed record to a label. The abstraction is useful, but it removes three features that make diagnostic reasoning difficult. First, evidence is ordered: a deficit followed by recovery is not equivalent to recovery followed by a deficit. Second, negative and missing findings are not interchangeable. Third, several hypotheses may remain live until a late observation changes their relative support. A model can score well on an ordinary held-out set while using the wrong temporal cue, collapsing under a new observation process, or becoming confidently wrong when the evidence is genuinely ambiguous.

Q-Neuro asks a deliberately narrow question: can a diagnosis-indexed state, updated by evidence-conditioned operators and read through interacting amplitudes, provide a useful computational bias for sequential differential diagnosis? The state is complex-valued, but the implementation is conventional PyTorch on a CPU. The word “quantum-inspired” refers only to mathematical motifs—complex amplitudes, noncommuting transformations, Hermitian actions, and density matrices—that already have substantial prior art. It does not imply quantum hardware, biological quantum effects, or a physical measurement process. Complex-valued networks and unitary recurrence are established machine-learning methods [@trabelsi2018; @arjovsky2016; @jing2017], and quantum-probability models have long represented order effects in judgment [@wang2014; @trueblood2012].

The scientific risk is obvious. A sufficiently expressive complex model can fit a synthetic benchmark, and a benchmark designed alongside a model can inadvertently reward that model. The project therefore treats every attractive mechanism as a hypothesis to be broken. The sequence begins with an unordered impossibility control, then adds parameter-matched real and two-channel controls, a validation-tuned GRU, multiple unseen generator worlds, ambiguity pairs, held-out compositions, active acquisition, eighteen dynamical laws, critical ablations, frozen probes, unconventional training laws, hard execution measurements, and evidence-level trajectories. The registry preserves superseded and failed runs rather than deleting them.

This design makes the central comparison sharper than “complex versus real.” A two-channel real network can store the same number of real scalars as a complex state and can use a paired sum-of-squares readout. A real operator state can preserve order without phase. A Hamiltonian-style model can retain coherent rotation while changing the parameterization. A density factor can guarantee a positive semidefinite, unit-trace diagnostic state. A GRU and a diagonal state-space model can test whether generic recurrence explains the effect. An MLP is intentionally unable to solve chronology twins whose aggregate evidence is identical. These controls turn a visually unusual architecture into a set of falsifiable contrasts.

The strongest positive result is a repeated within-simulator robustness effect. At 1,000 cases, complex dynamics are better than the two-channel real control across five unseen world seeds and four shift severities; the absolute paired advantage is 0.0627 under nuisance shift, 0.0609 under mild shift, 0.0602 under moderate shift, and 0.0538 under severe shift. The intervals use world seed as the replication unit after averaging training seeds. The critical ablation suite further shows that removing order composition, phase-sensitive measurement, or observed-negative evidence degrades the moderate-shift result. Those observations are consistent with a useful interaction among ordered recurrence, signed evidence, and complex measurement.

The negative results are equally important. At 250 source-world cases, the tuned GRU reaches 0.920 top-1 accuracy, compared with 0.774 for the real operator and 0.699 for the complex operator in the stronger-control study. On observationally identical ambiguity twins, the real operator assigns 0.836 probability mass to the two valid labels and obtains 1.148 pair NLL; the complex model assigns only 0.212 mass and obtains 2.581 NLL. Calibration fitted on the source world does not transport to shifted worlds. The hard-halting law saves executed states and latency only by sending every case through exactly two steps. The phase-gradient optimizer is slower than ordinary multi-objective AdamW without improving shifted accuracy. These failures rule out the broader story that complex hypothesis states are uniformly better, intrinsically uncertain, naturally adaptive, or unusually interpretable.

The contribution is best understood as an experimental instrument. NeuroWorld exposes causal chronology, factor structure, missingness, observation noise, and world shifts in a form that allows exact counterfactuals and leakage checks. The registry links each claim to configuration, seeds, environment, metrics, paired analysis, and figures. A claim ledger records supporting and counterevidence; a failure ledger records why plausible mechanisms did not work. The manuscript, tables, figures, dashboard, and Word/PDF artifacts are regenerated from the repository.

We make four bounded contributions. First, we provide a causal synthetic task family for sequential differential reasoning, including counterfactual chronology twins and independently seeded unseen worlds. Second, we implement a broad hypothesis-state comparison spanning conventional and quantum-inspired dynamics under approximately matched parameter budgets. Third, we report a replicated synthetic robustness effect for the complex operator together with ablations that identify necessary components under the tested design. Fourth, we show where the attractive interpretation fails: ambiguity, calibration transport, source-world sample efficiency, optimizer novelty, adaptive depth, and unique interpretability.

No patient data are used. No result establishes diagnostic safety, clinical utility, novelty in the legal or priority sense, or superiority over contemporary medical AI. The phrase “diagnosis” throughout refers to labels in a simulator. The work should be read as a rigorous model-development and hypothesis-testing study whose next scientific step is independent replication on generators not designed by the authors, followed—only if the effect survives—by privacy-preserving evaluation on governed real data.
