# Discussion

## What the result changes

The experiments support a modest but meaningful update. Treating a differential as an evolving, ordered hypothesis state can matter under causal shift, and the tested complex parameterization is more robust than its matched two-channel real control across the declared worlds. The effect survives interventions on commutativity, phase-sensitive measurement, and negative evidence. This makes the architecture worth independent study.

The result does not support the maximal story that began the project. Q-Neuro is not uniformly more data-efficient, calibrated, uncertain, adaptive, interpretable, or compute-efficient. A tuned GRU dominates the source low-data regime. A real operator dominates irreducible ambiguity. Conventional recurrent states expose simulator factors at least as well. Hard halting degenerates to fixed truncation. Phase-gradient optimization adds cost without frontier gain. The “revolution” is therefore methodological if anything: build a system that can efficiently disprove its own attractive mechanisms.

## Why complex dynamics may help under these shifts

One plausible explanation is parameter sharing across sign, order, and measurement. Evidence-specific complex actions rotate and scale a bounded state, while the quadratic readout couples real and imaginary channels. Under nuisance changes, this structure may preserve relational features that a GRU fits more opportunistically. The phase-insensitive and commutative ablations support this interpretation within the model.

Several alternatives remain. The complex optimizer geometry may simply regularize training; the real controls may be insufficiently matched; the simulator may encode temporal symmetries aligned with complex rotation; or the chosen hyperparameter budget may favor one family. None can be resolved by adding more worlds from the same generator. An independent reimplementation with real block-matrix equivalence controls and separately designed causal templates is the decisive next experiment.

## Accuracy–ambiguity tension

The most important conceptual failure is that high order discrimination does not imply a well-formed differential. The complex model learns sharp boundaries that are robust to the declared shifts, yet assigns little probability to the valid pair when observations are indistinguishable. This suggests that the magnitude-squared head and cross-entropy objective reward decisive classification more strongly than calibrated coexistence.

A revised model could separate evidential state from decision state. One branch would preserve a distribution over observational equivalence classes; another would resolve order when sufficient evidence appears. Density states might help only if off-diagonal structure receives a learning signal. Set-valued labels, proper scoring rules for partial identification, or conformal prediction could supply that signal. Any improvement must be evaluated against the real operator, which already performs much better on ambiguity.

## From observation to intervention

The trajectory audit shows measurable state revision and chronology-dependent separation, but it remains correlational. The next mechanism study should intervene. Candidate tests include reversing only event times, editing the negative-evidence operator, rotating a specific phase direction, clamping a probed factor, or ablating a readout component. A successful mechanism claim would predict how those interventions change probability and then verify the prediction on unseen worlds.

Active acquisition creates another intervention path. Rather than querying the feature that minimizes the model’s own entropy, a policy could estimate expected causal discrimination between the current top hypotheses and account for query cost. This requires a calibrated outcome model. The Transformer failure shows that expected information gain can amplify a misspecified predictor.

## Discovery engine findings

The automated discovery stage evaluates 36 candidates across architecture, training, and halting studies. It applies declared surprise rules and six-objective Pareto tests, yielding 26 flags and five proposals. The dominant surprises are not hidden wins: order-blind success, accuracy–ambiguity tension, generator-shift reversal, lack of a phase-optimizer frontier gain, and hard-halting degeneracy.

This is a healthier role for automated discovery than searching a large result table for the largest favorable number. The engine emits candidate proposals with counterevidence and confidence, but the data that select a proposal cannot confirm it. Its output is a queue for new preregistered studies.

## Near-term research program

The highest-priority experiment is independent causal replication. A second generator should preserve only the abstract requirements—signed evidence, temporal interventions, missingness, and ambiguous equivalence classes—while changing factor definitions, observation mechanics, and code ownership. The primary endpoint should be complex-minus-strongest-real moderate-shift top-1, with ambiguity NLL and calibration as co-primary safety metrics.

The second priority is ambiguity-aware learning. Compare set-valued cross-entropy, proper partial-identification scores, density-state supervision, and a real operator baseline. Success requires moving probability mass toward the valid set without materially reducing shifted accuracy.

The third priority is real-equivalence stress testing. Implement explicit block-real versions of every complex operation, match initialization and optimizer statistics, and test whether constraints rather than arithmetic produce the effect. If block-real equivalence erases the difference, the useful contribution becomes the structural constraint. If a gap remains under matched optimization, it deserves closer theoretical analysis.

Only after those steps should the project consider governed real-world data. The present repository supplies the reproducible baseline against which such work can fail clearly.
