# Discussion

The central result is not that the original experiment was numerically wrong. It is that its scientific interpretation depended on a non-equivalent comparator. Within NeuroWorld, the complex operator did outperform the historical two-channel real model. Once the exact tied real block is implemented, the computation is reproduced at numerical precision. Once the comparison is expanded to the strongest real model in each matched cell, the apparent advantage becomes non-positive throughout reduced pilot, discovery, and held-out evaluation.

This distinction matters beyond Q-Neuro. Structured architectures are often given carefully designed constraints while controls are represented by generic model names. Equal parameter counts do not make two hypothesis classes diagnostic of the same mechanism. An exact implementation-level control can be more informative than a larger collection of conventional baselines because it identifies whether the claimed arithmetic is necessary. Broader controls remain essential for the separate question of practical architecture performance.

The law experiment provides a second lesson. The quadratic relationship between order information, severity, and the performance gap looked compelling in discovery: R2 was 0.949 and MAE was 0.00260. Its held-out R2 of -30.94 shows how easily a smooth pattern over 12 aggregate cells can be mistaken for a general law. Sign transfer is uninformative here because every effect is non-positive. Freezing the coefficients and thresholds converts that seductive fit into a useful negative result.

The blocked grand experiment is also part of the evidence. Six missing requirements are not paperwork defects. Incomplete real controls, unequal search budgets, missing compute records, an incomplete shift grid, reduced discovery, and absent raw predictions each create a route by which a confirmatory number could be disputed or reinterpreted. The protocol therefore keeps the sealed benchmark untouched. A future team can complete the gates without inheriting a contaminated primary outcome.

There remains a narrower positive research direction. Complex coordinates may be a concise parameterization for phase-native data, or may induce useful optimization in settings not tested here. The real-block identity does not make parameterizations interchangeable under every optimizer and nonlinearity [@tan2022; @wu2023]. Such hypotheses should be phrased against exact mapped controls, measured on objectives beyond top-1 accuracy, and separated from claims of unique representational capacity.

Negative results are sometimes described as failures to discover. Here they are the discovery. The evidence shows exactly where a promising claim breaks: at comparator equivalence, at best-real selection, at held-out quantitative prediction, and at the boundary between a reduced study and a grand confirmation. The release is designed so that these boundaries are easier to cite than the original promotional interpretation.
